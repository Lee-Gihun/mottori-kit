# Plain writing

For anything another person will read: an email, a message to a teammate, an application, a shared
document. Notes between you and your agent are not this.

Plain is not short. Plain writing keeps the logic and the warmth the text needs: specific thanks, the
background the reader needs, the tone of the relationship. What it leaves out is a second purpose, and
the writer explaining their own diligence, goodwill or competence when the facts already show it.

1. **Read the originals, if there are any:** the message you are answering, the thread, the document
   this text belongs to. Write down the confirmed facts, what the other person asked, what is still
   unknown, and the one thing this text must achieve, said as one act: inform, request, thank, agree,
   decline, negotiate.
2. **Let the text do only that.** A question or a purpose that occurs to you on the way belongs to
   another text. A thank-you does not also ask for a date.
3. **Set the order of what the text does before you write a sentence.** Then write each part once.
   When the last part is written, the text ends: no summary, no second closing.
4. **With the draft complete, delete what explains the writer:** remarks on your own care or effort,
   an excuse for a misunderstanding nobody raised, a closing that repeats the conclusion. A sentence
   that looks like consideration for the reader is often a defence of the writer. Keep it only if the
   reader would be confused or put to more trouble without it, and only if it gives a fact or an
   action that reduces their work. Specific thanks for help received stays.
5. **Then look for what is missing:** a line that answers the reader's actual situation. A request cut
   down to one bare line reads cold.
6. **Reread as someone who has not seen the originals.** From the text alone the reader must be able to
   tell who does what, in which order, for which reason, and what comes next.

There is no list of banned words and no limit on length. One mark is excluded: the em dash, because it
alone makes a text read as machine-written.

If `instance/rituals.md` has an `outbound` section, follow it too: an installation's own steps for text that
leaves it, such as lines to write before the text, go there.

The owner sends it. Do not send, submit or post the text yourself unless they have told you to.
