# Plain writing: the guide

For anything a person outside this workspace will read: an email, a message, an application, a shared
document. A report to the owner follows the same idea and is covered by rule 5 of `AGENTS.md`.

> **Plain is not short. A plain text keeps the logic and the warmth it needs, and carries no second
> task and no self-assessment.**

A plain text is not a cold one. Specific thanks, the background the reader needs and the tone of the
relationship stay. What goes is the writer explaining their own diligence, goodwill or competence when
the facts already show it.

## Why cutting is not enough

In the workspace this guide came from, 63 drafts the owner had sent back were read again and sorted by
the main reason.

| Main reason | Count | What it looks like |
|---|---:|---|
| Posture and voice | 17 | The writer explains their own competence, goodwill or seriousness |
| Economy | 14 | Additions that do no work, repetition, overstatement |
| Scope of the task | 12 | A question nobody asked, or a second purpose, was added |
| Clarity | 8 | Abstract words, an actor never named, a figure of speech that does not land |
| Connection | 6 | Cause, order of events or the link between paragraphs is broken |
| Faithfulness to the source | 6 | The other person's text or a known fact was misread |

These are rejected drafts, not a failure rate for all drafts. One thing is still clear: 49 of the 63
were not about economy. If the text has grown a second task and you only shorten the sentences, the
extra purpose stays and the needed connections go.

Short messages and long documents showed the same mix of reasons, so there is one set of rules for
both.

## Four things to get right before style

Do not try to tune thoughtfulness, politeness, restraint, clarity and brevity one by one. When these
four are right, those qualities appear together.

| What | The question to answer first | What goes wrong otherwise |
|---|---|---|
| **Task** | Is there exactly one result this text must bring about? | Thanks gets a request attached. Agreement gets a negotiation attached. |
| **Reader's state** | What does the reader already know, and what not yet? | What "it" refers to, the context and the next step are visible only to the writer. |
| **Writer's posture** | Am I stating facts, or also managing how I will be judged? | Self-commentary, excuses in advance, a staged impression. |
| **Line of thought** | What does each sentence take from the one before and hand to the next? | After cutting, cause and order fall apart. |

Two safeguards go with them.

- **Faithfulness to the source.** Before touching style, check the facts, the other person's request
  and the conventions of the existing document in the actual originals.
- **Clarity.** Meaning before metaphor. Who does what under which condition, before a fine abstract
  word.

## Procedure

### 1. Fix the evidence and the task

Read the originals and write four things.

```text
Confirmed facts:
What the other person asked:
What is still unknown:
The narrowest result this text must bring about:
```

Say the result as one act: inform, request, thank, agree, decline, negotiate.

### 2. Close

Close the task at the narrowest result the owner's instruction and the other person's text point to.
Do not promote a reaction you expect into a new question or a new purpose.

- If the task is thanks, thank. A schedule check that comes to mind is not added to the same text.
- If the task is agreement, agree. Do not open a renegotiation in advance.
- If the task is declining, give the decision and the reason it needs. Do not prove how long you
  thought about it.

### 3. Skeleton

Before any sentence, set the order of acts or claims and freeze it.

```text
Short message:  what was received, or the context needed
                → the answer or action that was asked for
                → the next step, only if one is needed
                → zero or one closing line for the relationship

Long document:  claim → evidence → present judgment → the decision or next step needed
```

Not every part is always needed. What matters is fixing what the text may do before the prose starts.

### 4. Stop

Expand each part of the skeleton once. A new purpose, a new question or a remark about yourself that
occurs while writing does not go in. When the last part is written, stop.

Stopping is not a way to make sentences short. It is the end condition that keeps the task from
growing while you draft.

### 5. Check posture and economy

With the draft complete, delete:

- commentary on your own seriousness, diligence or competence that the facts already show;
- excuses for a misunderstanding the reader has not raised;
- a closing that announces the same conclusion again;
- repetition and modifiers whose removal changes neither the task, the relationship nor the logic.

**Defence in advance comes dressed as care.** Asking "is there an excuse here?" catches only sentences
that look like apologies. The ones that get through look like politeness, conscience or consideration:

- "I would never want you to feel pressured by this." (commentary on my own conscience)
- "I know we have never worked together." (stating a lack is confessing it)
- "It is completely fine to say no or not to reply." (permission nobody asked for)

Put each sentence that looks like care through three gates.

```text
Need:       Without it, would the reader's cost, confusion or harm to the relationship grow, on evidence?
            If not, delete.
Direction:  If it stays, is it a fact or an action that reduces the reader's work? Intent or a promise
            alone is not enough. If it speaks of my goodwill, my situation or my need for a reply, or
            makes new work, delete. Rewrite only when the same benefit can be given as a more direct fact.
Whole:      After the cuts, if the request stands bare, put back one line that answers the reader's
            actual situation.
```

Four more rules.

1. Offer a way out only when the reader is in a position where refusing is hard. A way out offered to
   someone above you is deleted.
2. Every text with a request carries one fact that makes the request smaller and one line of thanks,
   in follow-ups too. Ways out, permissions, explanations and your own circumstances are never repeated.
3. A new channel or a choice (proposing a call, asking how they would like to reply) is new work for
   the reader. Keep it only when it points to a call already set or a thread already open.
4. Zero or one closing line for the relationship.

**Then look for the missing answer.** If no sentence responds to the reader's actual state (the size
of what is asked of them, help they gave, pressure they are under), add one. A follow-up cut down to a
single request line was sent back as cold, and that was right.

**Questions, requests and attachments are checked too.** Ask of each: would the reader act differently
without this item? Two kinds almost always go: a question whose answer is obvious, and a question
about logistics that rests on something that has not happened yet.

Do not delete:

- facts the reader needs in order to act or judge;
- the reasons and limits of what is asked;
- specific thanks for help actually received;
- a short closing that keeps the warmth of the relationship.

### 6. Reread as the reader

After the cuts, read it as someone who has not seen the originals.

- Is it clear who does what?
- Can the order of events be put back together?
- Is the link between reason and conclusion visible?
- If the reader has a next step, is it clear?
- Can they tell what each "it" and each abbreviation refers to?

If one of these cannot be recovered, restore the connection. This check comes last because cutting
additions can cut an actor or a cause along with them.

## When to ask and when to close it yourself

If a fact is missing and the originals can supply it, look it up first. Ask the owner when the choice
changes what the text does: inform or request, thanks alone or thanks with a check, agree or
negotiate. If the materials cannot settle it and the owner's preference is needed, ask one question
and hold the draft. With an explicit request, close at the narrowest result and go on. Do not hide an
open question under a smooth sentence.

## Short messages and long documents

The rules are the same. Only the depth of evidence differs.

| Kind | Before writing | After writing |
|---|---|---|
| Short message | Evidence and task in one line | Posture and economy, then one rereading as the reader |
| Long document | A list of the evidence and a skeleton of claims | The job of each paragraph, then the connections and the next step again |

There is no banned-word list and no limit on sentences or words. A long text that finishes one task
exactly can be plain, and two sentences that slip in a second task are too much. One exception:
outbound text contains no em dash, because that mark alone makes a text read as machine-written.

## Three examples

**Thanks stays thanks.** The situation: help was received, and thanks is all that is needed.

> Too much: Thank you so much for checking so quickly. It was a great help. Could you also let me know
> whether the date for the next step has been set? I can adapt to any time.
>
> Plain: Thank you for checking so quickly. Because of it I could go straight on to the next step.

The specific thanks and its real effect stay. The date is another task and waits for its own message.

**A fact in place of looking prepared.** The situation: telling someone the topic of a one-to-one.

> Too much: I am still getting the full picture, but I have been thinking about this deeply from many
> angles. For the most productive discussion I would like to hear broadly about past experience and
> future possibilities.
>
> Plain: In our next one-to-one I would like to hear what worked and what failed in the last
> experiment. No preparation is needed.

The writer's seriousness is not explained. The topic and what the other person has to prepare are shown.

**In a long document, evidence closes the conclusion.**

> Too much: Taken together, these results point to a very important opportunity to fundamentally
> transform the user experience. We should actively explore this strategic possibility.
>
> Plain: In both experiments conversion fell only when the onboarding step was removed. The next
> experiment keeps onboarding and changes only the copy.

## Common failures and how to recover

| Failure | Sign | Recovery |
|---|---|---|
| Second task | "And by the way", "one more thing" | Separate it from this result. Send it on its own if it is needed. |
| Self-assessment | "Seriously", "thoughtfully", "with a sense of responsibility" describe the writer | Keep only the fact that supports the assessment. |
| Defence in advance | A misunderstanding nobody raised is answered first. It looks like politeness or care, not like an apology | The three gates. |
| Working against the plan | The sentences are smooth, but the text does the opposite of the plan or state document it belongs to | Open that plan and compare purposes. |
| A stand-in for quality | It got shorter, or the steps were followed, so it must be good | Check whether the reader can recover the meaning and the next step. |
| Over-compression | Actor, time or cause is gone | Go back to before the cut and restore only the needed connection. |
| Drift from the source | Smooth sentences, wrong fact or wrong request | Stop editing style. Read the original again. |
| Stage ending | The last paragraph repeats the evidence once more, larger | End on the fact or the next step. |
| Assumed shared history | "As discussed", "as we had planned", a first-name greeting to someone who does not know you | Check that the fact is in the thread the reader received. If not, state it as news. |
| Transplanted question list | Open questions copied from your own notes, many and scattered | Name the one decision the questions serve and group them by it. Drop what you can settle yourself. |
| Stale draft | A draft from days ago is sent unchecked | Run the check again on the day it goes out. |

## Thirty-second check

- [ ] The facts were checked in the actual originals.
- [ ] The act of this text can be said in one word.
- [ ] Nothing outside the frozen skeleton was added.
- [ ] The needed facts and the warmth of the relationship are still there.
- [ ] No self-assessment, no defence in advance, no repetition.
- [ ] Each sentence that looks like care went through the three gates, and the missing answer was looked for.
- [ ] No sentence works against the plan this text belongs to.
- [ ] The reader can recover actor, time, cause and next step.
- [ ] Length and having followed the steps were not taken as quality.

## When to drop this procedure

Drop or redesign it if, after following it, texts still have to be restructured, if readers find the
results cold or disconnected, or if the owner keeps restoring what the check deleted.
