# Deep pass

For one hard question. It ends in one recommendation the owner can check.

Use it when the owner asks for depth, or when a decision cannot be undone, reaches people outside, is
expensive, or rests on one thing nobody has checked. A light question gets a light answer.

Seven steps. Merge, shorten or skip steps when the question allows, and say which you skipped.

1. **State the problem.** The question, the result wanted, and what would show that the work failed.
   Keep four things apart: who reads the answer, who approves it, who should be better off, and what
   the work is trying to improve.
2. **Read first.** Open the current files and the owner's own words before you think of options. Say
   what you could not read and how old your evidence is.
3. **Look from more than one side.** Form a first answer, then pick the viewpoints that could give a
   different one: the `lenses` skill has thirteen, and one you make up for this problem counts the
   same. Note the viewpoint you did not take that could matter most.
4. **Try to break the answer you favour.** Use whichever fits: a boundary case, what you would see if
   the opposite were true, or one small case worked to the end. Write down what you expect before you
   check.
5. **Decide.** One recommendation. For the strongest option you rejected, state its best argument and
   the condition under which it would win.
6. **Test it on a fresh reader.** Write a few questions the reader should be able to answer from your
   text, with the answers you expect. Give the text and the questions, and nothing else, to a reader
   who has not seen the work: a new session, or a person. Where their answers differ from yours, fix
   the text.
7. **Say the limits.** What you found wrong and what is still unknown. If the decision is of a kind
   named at the top of this page, ask a model from another vendor to attack the recommendation, provided
   the owner allows that vendor to see the material. If that review did not happen, say so.

Come back with five things, and make nothing up to fill one:

1. One recommendation.
2. The strongest objection, and your answer to it.
3. The options you dropped, and why.
4. What is still unknown, including what you could not read, the viewpoint you did not take, and any
   step or review you skipped.
5. What would reverse the answer.
